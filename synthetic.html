Chapter 01

Who are these patients?

Every patient in this study is a newborn baby with Single Ventricle Disease (SVD). The central clinical event is "Sudden Hypoxemia" — a life-threatening drop in oxygen caused by a blocked shunt.

Quick Stat

Sudden hypoxemia happened in 14 of 157 babies (8.9%). Predicting it 30 minutes early is the goal of this AI research.

Chapter 02

The Two Datasets

Research uses Dataset 1 (Electronic Health Records) and Dataset 2 (ICU Monitor signals recorded at 2 samples per second).

Chapter 03

Physiological Signals

Monitoring includes SpO2 (Oxygen), HR (Heart Rate), and ABP (Arterial Blood Pressure). These signals are used to identify "Type A" (gradual) vs "Type B" (abrupt) events.

Chapter 04

Machine Learning Models

A CatBoost model outperformed traditional Logistic Regression, reaching an AUC of 0.80. The most important predictor was Shunt Size.

Chapter 05

Signal Plots

Real-world signal recordings revealed that Type B events (60%) have almost no warning, whereas Type A events (40%) show a "prodromal drift" hours before.

Chapter 06

Why Synthetic Data?

Real patient data is limited and protected by HIPAA. To test the Clinical Decision Review dashboard with real doctors, we needed 100 diverse, synthetic "digital twin" scenarios.

Chapter 07

Building Dataset 1

We created clinical charts for 100 synthetic patients. This ensures the statistical patterns match the real cohort while maintaining zero risk of identifying a real child.

Chapter 08

Building Dataset 2

The time-series data was built using an AR(1) model to ensure heart rates and oxygen levels change realistically over time, rather than jumping randomly.
